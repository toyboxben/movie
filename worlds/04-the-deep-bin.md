# 04 — THE DEEP BIN

**Seq 06. The Ordeal.**
**Category: failed, broken, and abandoned prints.**

---

## What it is

Under everything, in the dark, is where the things that didn't work end up. Not a
graveyard — nobody here is dead. A **waiting room**.

The Bin is a vast dim space beneath the Bed, and its floor is a deep drift of discarded
work: warped bases, snapped supports, figures missing limbs, models printed at the wrong
scale, and the tangled fused clumps of prints that collapsed into knots. The drift is
hundreds of feet deep in places and it shifts underfoot.

The Bin-Lost live here. They are broken, they are half-built, and they are **gentle**.
They do not want anything. They are not hostile and they do not menace. They come and look
at you, quietly, and go back to waiting, and that is far more upsetting than a monster
would be. Several of them are unmistakably things a child designed and abandoned.

One shaft of pale light comes down from very far above, and nobody goes near it.

## Story function

This is the low point and every beat in it hurts.

- **June's origin.** This is where she is from. She has been climbing out of here for
  years.
- **Her name.** The maker's mark on her heel. `JUNIPER`, four letters built. She has never
  told anyone.
- **The Finisher takes Colossus** (SH-070). Gently. As a mercy. In front of Kit, because a
  half-made thing is the most obvious suffering in the room, and he says so kindly, and he
  is not entirely wrong.
- **Kit freezes.** He tries to draw a rescue and cannot commit to the line — starts,
  stops, erases, restarts, and the moment passes while he is still deciding. The failure
  is not that he isn't strong enough. It is that he does the exact thing he always does.
- **The Tower loses four layers.** The survivors go quiet for the first time in the film.
- **The admission.** Kit says out loud, to June, who is the worst possible person to hear
  it, that he has never let anyone have anything he made.
- **June's answer is not comfort.** It's a deadline.

## The beat that could only happen here

Kit is standing in a room full of every abandoned thing, being told by an abandoned thing
what he is, while abandoning another one. The location *is* the argument. Nowhere else in
the film does the setting make the case on its own.

## Key light

**A single pale shaft from directly above,** very far away, hitting the floor in one
place. Everything else is bounce and the faint glow from the Tower's slots. Near
monochrome — this is the only sequence in the film that is essentially desaturated, and
the colour returns the moment they leave.

## The Bin-Lost — direction

They must never be staged as a threat. No looming, no reaching, no crowding, no sudden
movement. They approach slowly, stop at a polite distance, look, and stay. If a shot of
them reads as scary rather than sad, it is wrong and should be regenerated.

Two of them should be visibly **hand-designed originals** like June — clearly somebody's
own creation, clearly abandoned. Never pointed at. One of them should be looking at June.

## No Hand, no horizon

The Deep Bin is the only exterior-scale location with **no view of the Hand and no visible
Smooth horizon**. You are underneath everything down here. That absence is why the sequence
feels airless, and it is also why the return to daylight in Seq 07 hits.

## Sound

Enormous dead air with a very long, very dry reverb tail. Occasional far-off settling of
the drift. A low sub that sits under everything and is felt rather than heard. When the
Finisher arrives, the sub cuts out — the mix goes thin and small and there is nowhere to
hide.
