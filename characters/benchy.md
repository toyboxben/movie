# BENCHY — Mentor

**The most-printed object in the universe. A small tugboat. Extremely tired.**

---

## Who he is

Every printer ever made prints a Benchy first. It is the calibration boat, the universal
test object, the thing you make to find out whether your machine works. Which means there
are more Benchys than anything else in existence, and this one has met a great many of
himself, and has opinions about it.

He is the ferryman. He runs the rivers between worlds because he is one of the few things
built to actually float, and he has been doing it long enough that he has stopped finding
any of it remarkable. Terse, weathered, correct. He is not a warm mentor — he does not
encourage Kit, he *assesses* him, and the one time he says something kind it lands like a
truck because he has spent six minutes not saying it.

**Function:** delivers the theme, refuses to solve the problem, and survives. The film
does not need to kill the mentor, and not killing him is the better subversion.

**Contradiction that makes him work:** he is the most common object in the universe and
he is the only one who thinks originality matters.

## The theme line

Midpoint, on the river, not looking at him:

> "There's four million of me. Every one of us came out the same, and not one of us came
> out by accident. Somebody pressed the button. Nothing here is done. That's not a problem.
> People still use us. That's the whole thing."

Said once. Never referenced again. Kit does not acknowledge it, and we do not cut to his
face to check whether he heard.

**Silhouette key:** squat hull, one square wheelhouse, one stack. Instantly readable.
Anyone who has run a 3D printer will recognize him in the first frame, which is a free
gift to our audience.

## Reference set

- Turnaround at water level: front, 3/4, profile, rear
- Porthole states: dark, dim glow, bright glow (his "expression" range)
- Hull tilts: level, forward pitch (assertive), backward pitch (skeptical), hard list (alarm)
- Contexts: floating on filament river, beached, in dock, in dry storage
- Detail crop: chipped bow, waterline residue, layer lines in raking light

## STATE CHANGES

**None in the pilot.** Benchy appears at SH-017 and is unchanged through SH-047, his last
shot. He does not go into the Deep Bin and he does not get scarred — a boat is one of the
few things in this world that can simply leave, and him being fine while everybody else
accumulates damage is a quiet joke that pays off if the series continues.

The scarring reference sheet in `turnarounds.md §4d` is generated anyway, for episode two.

## Voice

**Direction:** gravel, weathered, economical. Late-fifties dockworker who has said
everything he has to say twice already. Never raises his voice, never speeds up. Comedy
comes from how little he gives. Think of a harbor pilot annoyed at being asked directions.

**Lip sync tier:** C, always. He has no mouth by design. His porthole glows on speech,
which is done with a light animation in post if needed and never depends on the model.
This makes him the cheapest speaking character in the film — put exposition on him.
