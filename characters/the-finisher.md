# THE FINISHER — Antagonist

**The first thing anyone ever printed. It came out wrong. It was thrown away.
It has spent every day since making sure nothing is ever wrong again.**

---

## Who he is

Tall, slender, seamless, and mirror-smooth — a featureless white human figure with no
face, moving with unhurried grace. He is the only object in the Toybox with no layer
lines, which in this world is not elegance, it is a wound.

He is never angry, never loud, and never threatens anybody. He speaks with genuine
warmth, the way a very good hospice nurse speaks, and he believes completely that he is
performing mercy. Every printed thing carries the visible evidence of its own flaws
across its entire body, permanently, for everyone to see. He finds this unbearable on
their behalf. So he smooths them: layer lines first, then colour, then features, until
they are blank and still and finally, mercifully, done.

**The horror is that he is kind.** If a scene has him raising his voice or gloating,
that scene is wrong.

## The reveal

He was the first test print. A human figure, attempted before anyone knew how. The face
failed — collapsed into blankness mid-print. He was pulled off the bed and discarded, and
what got made second, and third, and four million times after, all came out better than
him.

He is not trying to destroy the world. He is trying to make the world match him, because
if everything is smooth then nothing was ever wrong, and if nothing was ever wrong then
neither was he.

**He is Kit's flaw with a body.** Kit hides things in a drawer so they can never be
judged. The Finisher has industrialised that instinct: if nothing is ever seen unfinished,
nothing was ever wrong — and nothing gets to be enjoyed either.

## What he did

He stopped the Hand. That's the crime at the centre of the plot, revealed in Seq 05: the
Hand did not break, it was *cut*, by him, because a new print is a new imperfect thing and
he has been quietly ending those for as long as anyone can remember. He is not trying to
destroy the world. He is trying to stop it from producing any more suffering.

He does not know, and never learns, that the thing the Hand was building is a dog.

## The planted object

A small blank white figurine sits half-buried in the pen cup on Kit's desk in Seq 01.
Never remarked on, never lit specially. In Seq 08 the cup holds only pens. That is the
entire sting, and nobody says a word about it.

## What smoothing looks like

A consistent, escalating visual grammar. Get this right and the film has a monster; get it
inconsistent and it has a filter.

1. The rings fade — the surface goes glassy and wrong
2. Colour drains from the extremities inward
3. Features soften — eyes close over, mouths seal
4. Motion slows to nothing. The figure stops mid-gesture.
5. It stands there. Perfect. Finished.

They are not dead. That is worse. Late in the episode a smoothed figure very slightly
turns its head, and nobody mentions it.

**Prompt phrase for the smoothing effect — paste verbatim:**
```
its printed layer striations dissolving into a glassy featureless surface, colour draining
from the extremities inward toward the centre, features softening and sealing over into
blank white, motion slowing to a complete stop mid-gesture
```

They are not dead. That is worse. In SH-064 a smoothed figure very slightly turns its head
in the far background, and no character reacts and the camera does not push in.

**Appearances:** SH-063 (Gearhaven, introduces himself), SH-064 (leaves), SH-069 and
SH-071 (takes Colossus), SH-076 and SH-077 (the confrontation and the offer). Six shots
total — he is barely in the film, which is why he works.

## PROMPT BLOCK — paste verbatim into every shot

```
THE FINISHER: a tall slender featureless humanoid figure, roughly seven feet tall in a
world of small toys, moulded from perfectly seamless matte-white material with absolutely
no layer lines, no seams, no texture and no visible joints anywhere on his body. Smooth
blank oval head with no face at all — no eyes, no mouth, no nose, only an unbroken curved
surface with a faint pearlescent sheen. Long elegant limbs, narrow shoulders, elongated
smooth hands with fingers slightly too long. He wears nothing. He stands and moves with
patient, unhurried, almost gentle grace, always upright, never hunched. He casts no
visible shadow and light falls on him flatly with no rim highlight, so he never quite
looks attached to the scene around him.
```

**Silhouette key:** tall, narrow, faceless, upright, blank. The negative space of every
other character in the film.

**Continuity traps:**
- He must have **zero** layer striations. Models will add texture to match the world — reject any keyframe where he has them.
- He must have **no rim light**, even in scenes where everything else does. This breaks
  the lighting law on purpose. Prompt it explicitly every time.
- He must never emote physically. No leaning in, no reaching menacingly, no head cock.
  Upright and calm in every frame.

## Reference set

- Turnaround: front, 3/4, profile, rear, full body, neutral stance
- The head, isolated, at multiple angles — establishing there is genuinely nothing there
- Hands: at his sides, one extended palm-up in offering (his only gesture), touching a toy
- Contexts: in The Smooth's white void (nearly invisible, only edge-defined), against the
  colour of a normal world (violently out of place), backlit in a doorway
- Scale reference: standing over Wren, standing among small toys
- Detail: the pearlescent sheen, the total absence of layer striations in raking light

## STATE CHANGES

None. He does not change. He cannot. That is his whole problem, and holding him
absolutely constant across 100 shots while everyone else accumulates damage is a
storytelling choice, not just a continuity convenience.

## Voice

**Direction:** warm, unhurried, genuinely kind. Mid-range, soft, with real affection in
it. He is delighted to meet Wren. He is sorry about the pain. He never raises his voice,
never speeds up, never uses a threatening word. Every line should be deliverable, without
changing a syllable, by someone tucking a child into bed.

The single hardest note for the actor: **he is not performing kindness. He means it.**

**Lip sync tier:** A — but inverted. He has no mouth, so there is nothing to sync, and the
disconnect between a warm human voice and a blank motionless face is the most unsettling
thing in the film. Use static, perfectly still shots of his face while he speaks at
length. The stillness does the work.

## Signature lines

- "Oh — look at you. You've got no rings at all." (his first line, to Kit, delighted)
- "It doesn't hurt. That's the part nobody believes. It has never once hurt."
- "You know exactly what I'm talking about. You've got a drawer full of it."
- "I was first. Do you understand what that means? There was nothing to compare me to. And then there was."
- On taking Colossus, tenderly: "Sweetheart. Look at you. Somebody stopped."
- Final line, entirely without malice, as Kit draws: "That's going to come out crooked."
